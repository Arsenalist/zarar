## Summary

Hello Muslim is a question-and-answer assistant that gives visitors a reliable, plain-spoken source of information about Islam, what it means to be a Muslim and clear up misconceptions, answering only from the material you have curated. It never draws on general internet knowledge. Every answer can be pitched at the reader's level, from teenager to historian, with a general level as the default. When a question falls outside your material, it says so and points the visitor to resources you have chosen.

It lives on the Hello Muslim website, which I will build alongside it. It works in any browser, including on phones, from day one. You can add, edit and remove content yourself through an admin screen, without involving me, saving you maintenance costs. The whole build takes six weeks or fewer.

## How it works

Your question-and-answer material is the only source the assistant answers from.

1. A visitor types a question in their own words.
2. The assistant finds the closest matches in your material. It understands meaning, so "do Muslims worship a different God" matches your entry on what the word Allah means.
3. If there is a strong match, it writes a reply built only from those entries, pitched at the audience level the visitor has chosen, and shows the source questions underneath, so the visitor can read your approved wording.
4. If there is no strong match, it does not guess. It shows a short note saying that this topic is not covered in your material, naming the topic so the visitor knows it understood them, and offers links from your curated resources list.

Requests for a personal religious ruling, questions about politics or sectarian disputes, and anything else you have not written material for fall in step 4 by design. The assistant never produces an answer on those topics.

**Audience levels.** By default the assistant answers from a general perspective. A visitor can choose a level that suits them, for example teen, young adult, or historian, and every answer from then on is written at that level. You decide which levels to offer and what each one means.

The technology underneath is a standard approach called retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Your material is stored in a searchable database, and a language model is used to match questions and phrase replies, under strict instructions to use nothing but your entries.

## What you get

- **The Hello Muslim website.** Home, About, Ask, Resources and Privacy pages, designed for Hello Muslim and built to load quickly on phones.
- **The assistant on the website, and on any other site you choose.** The assistant on the website's page, and on any other website that you may want the assistant to appear on.
- **Answers pitched to the reader.** Visitors pick the level that suits them, from teen to historian, and the assistant keeps that choice for their visit.
- **An admin screen you run yourself.** Add, edit and remove question-and-answer entries. Curate the resources list, and changes go live within a minute.
- **A view of how people actually use it.** Every question asked, what it was answered with, and whether the visitor found it useful (thumbs up or down). Every question that found no answer is recorded word for word and shown grouped by topic, so you can see exactly what people are looking for and add or reword entries to match.
- **A nightly link check.** Any resource link that stops working for any reason is flagged in the admin screen, so you know what needs an update.

This design keeps your maintenance cost low. As every question and outcome is recorded, you see the success and failure cases directly and can adjust your material to match what visitors ask, without guessing.

## How it improves over time

The assistant gets better from real use.

- **Every unsuccessful search is kept.** When a visitor's question finds no match, the exact wording is saved. In the admin screen you see these grouped by topic, most asked first, with the words visitors actually used. Next to each group you have three choices: write a new entry, attach the wording to an existing entry so it matches next time, or mark it as not for us.
- **Suggested entries.** For each group of unanswered questions, the assistant drafts a candidate entry from your existing material for you to approve, edit or discard. Nothing goes live without you.
- **It learns how people phrase things.** When a visitor's wording finds the right entry and they give it a thumbs up, that wording is saved as an alternative phrasing on the entry. Future visitors who ask the same way are matched more reliably. The facts in the entry never change this way.
- **Thumbs-down replies come with a suggested fix.** Each one appears in your review list with the entry it used and a note on what probably went wrong: a missing entry, an unclear one, or two that overlap.
- **A weekly digest.** Most asked topics, unanswered topics, and anything waiting for your review, in one short email.

## Marketing and revenue

Every question visitors ask is a record of what people want to know about Islam, and that record can attract visitors, raise donations, support grant applications and open doors to institutions. Some of that is built in, and some is listed as future possibilities, so you can see where this could go.

### Built in

- **A public page for every entry.** Each question in your material gets its own permanent page showing your approved answer, a switch between audience levels, related questions and a box to ask something else, so someone searching Google or an AI search tool lands on your answer instead of someone else's. Editing an entry updates its page within a minute.
- **Shareable answer cards.** Every answer has a share button that produces a clean image card sized for Instagram and WhatsApp, with the question, a short answer and a link back to the full page.
- **A donation prompt at the moment of help.** When a visitor gives an answer a thumbs up, they see a short note inviting them to support Hello Muslim, linking to your donation page. Each answer costs about a cent, so "$30 keeps 3,000 questions answered" is literally true.
- **A speaking link.** On topics you choose, the assistant offers to bring the conversation to the visitor's campus or workplace, linking to your speaking page.
- **A free embed for partner websites.** Mosques, interfaith groups, campus chaplaincies and schools can add the assistant to their own sites with one line of code. It carries a "Powered by Hello Muslim" link, partners cannot change your content, and the usage view shows which partner sites the questions came from.
- **Impact numbers for funders.** The weekly digest and the admin screen show questions answered, visits, the share of answers marked helpful and the most asked topics, and a one-page monthly summary can be downloaded for reports and grant applications.
- **A pre-launch question page.** The website goes up in the first week with a single invitation: send us the question you have always wanted to ask about Islam. The questions that arrive show you what to write about and are added to the test set.

### Future possibilities

None of the following is included in the price or timeline, but is possible with the ground work we'll lay.

**Paid versions for institutions.** Universities, school districts, hospitals, workplace inclusion teams and interfaith councils already pay for training on Islam. A version of the assistant on their own intranet or learning platform, with audience levels written for their people, for example "teacher", "nurse" or "HR manager", is something they could license yearly. 

**Google Ad Grants.** As a registered nonprofit, Hello Muslim can apply for up to $10,000 a month of free Google search advertising. The entry pages are exactly what those ads should point to: someone searching "is jihad holy war" sees your answer at the top. The program has rules that need monthly attention to stay eligible, so setup and management would be a separate piece of work.

**Nonprofit cloud credits.** As a registered nonprofit, Hello Muslim can also apply for DigitalOcean's nonprofit credits of up to $2,500, which would cover the hosting for the first year.

**Sponsored campaigns.** A foundation or business could sponsor a themed series, for example 30 questions for Ramadan, with its own answer cards and a page on the site.

## Your part

The assistant is only as good as the material behind it, and this is where most of your time is required.

**During the build**

- Hand over your question-and-answer material in whatever format you have it (spreadsheet, document).
- Approve the test set and review the test results (explained in the next section).
- Choose the audience levels you want offered, describe each one in a sentence, and review sample answers at every level.
- Review and approve the wording the assistant uses when a topic is not covered.
- Build the initial resources list: the reputable links you want visitors pointed to, with a one-line description each.

**After launch**

- First month: a few hours a week reviewing unanswered questions, flagged answers and thumbs-down replies, and adding or editing entries.
- After that: an hour or two a week for the same.
- Keep the resources list current when the nightly check flags a broken link.

## Testing and acceptance

Visitors never phrase a question the way your material does, so we test with questions written the way visitors would write them. This is the part I need your help with most.

**How it works.** Suppose one of your entries is "What does the word jihad actually mean?" A visitor might instead type any of these:

| What a visitor might type | Should find |
| --- | --- |
| is jihad holy war | the jihad entry |
| jihad meaning | the jihad entry |
| are muslims required to fight non believers | the jihad entry |

I will draft about 250 such test questions across your topics, each paired with the entry it should find. You review the list and correct it, because you know how your audience actually asks, which spellings they might use, and which questions could reasonably point to multiple entries.

We then ask the assistant all 250 questions and mark each answer right or wrong. You get the list. I adjust the assistant and repeat until it passes a bar you set.

**Audience levels are checked by eye.** For a sample of entries, you read the same answer at each level and confirm the facts match your material and the tone suits the reader. A teen answer must not lose accuracy, and a historian answer must not gain facts you did not write.

**The bar for launch.** Out of every 100 test questions, the assistant must find the right entry on the first try at least 90 times. In other words, nearly always right, and almost never off the mark.

The pilot is accepted when it clears that bar on the test set. Before we go live you try it yourself, for as long as you like, and send me anything that looks wrong.

## Mobile app

I recommend launching on the web only and deciding on a native app after 60 days of real usage.

- The assistant works in every phone browser from day one, and visitors can add it to their home screen like an app.
- A native app doubles what has to be built and maintained, for the same answers.
- Every change to an app waits on Apple and Google review, often days. A fix to the website is live in minutes.

An Android and iPhone app can follow as a second phase, quoted at that point. It would reuse everything built here.

## Privacy

Visitors ask questions anonymously. No accounts are created and no personal information is collected. Each question is sent to an AI provider to be matched and answered, and this is stated on the site's privacy page. For partner embeds, the system records which website a question came from, never who asked it.

**Hosted in the EU.** The server, database and question logs will live in DigitalOcean's Amsterdam or Frankfurt data centre. The main reason is EU data protection laws (GDPR) which are the strictest widely enforced privacy regime, so visitors' questions are held to that standard by default, and the logs stay under EU jurisdiction, which matters for a community that may be wary of how its questions could be accessed elsewhere.
